NltkNet Windows runtime - Microsoft redistribution review

The bundled Microsoft DLLs are NOT licensed under NltkNet's MIT license.
Their original binary contents are retained from the pinned CPython and NumPy
distributions. microsoft-redistribution.json records versions, SHA-256 hashes
and upstream terms. NumPy's wheel renames its MSVCP DLL; its contents are not
modified by NltkNet.

The VS 2022 Community terms are included verbatim as extracted from Microsoft's
hash-verified official document. This inclusion does NOT grant a Visual Studio
license to the publisher, accept an agreement for anyone, or assert compliance.
Use the terms applicable to your actual licensed edition.

VS 2022 Community's individual-use clause and OSI-open-source development clause
provide possible no-cost licensing routes. Section 4 covers Distributable Code,
with a link to the REDIST list, and permits third-party distribution as part
of applications subject to its conditions. The Microsoft redistribution
documentation limits redistribution to licensed Visual Studio users.

Before public publication the publisher must:
1. Confirm a valid applicable Visual Studio license and entitlement covering
   the runtime files, including the copies obtained through upstream wheels.
   Do not assume that merely using a GitHub-hosted Windows runner grants
   redistribution rights to the account publishing a NuGet package.
2. Verify the applicable REDIST list and original binary form for each DLL.
   The reviewed filenames are vcruntime140.dll, vcruntime140_1.dll and NumPy's
   hashed-name msvcp140 DLL; inspect the exact inventory versions and hashes.
3. Comply with section 4's significant-primary-functionality and downstream
   distributor/end-user protective-terms requirements and restrictions.
   Merely linking to or including this agreement is not proof of acceptance
   of sufficiently protective terms by downstream recipients.
4. Preserve applicable third-party terms in application distributions.
   Do not represent the Microsoft DLLs as MIT/open-source components.

The Windows NuGet requests license acceptance, but not every NuGet client
enforces an interactive acceptance step. Application distributors must arrange
the required recipient terms; the NuGet flag alone does not establish this.

This is the bundled, no-extra-runtime-installation route. Until the publisher
confirms the above, publication remains blocked. No external VC++ installer
is downloaded or launched on library initialization.
